I

Discovery

Problem discovery

TULIP began with a promise in Parliament. Making it work was voluntary for cities, so the platform had to earn its use.

A minister told Parliament that the education ministry would launch a portal with 100,000 internships for college students. AICTE, a body under the education ministry, built that portal. The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs joined it to post internships in the urban sector. These counted toward the AICTE total, under the name The Urban Learning Internship Program (TULIP).

TULIP was not a mandated program. States and cities did not have to post internships. The ministry wanted cities to see the value on their own: fresh graduates bring new ideas, and that builds the capacity of city governments.

When I joined in Aug 2020, the pilot had already been tested. The product had two sides:

User research

I interviewed every stakeholder I could reach, from interns to city officials. Three problems held TULIP back.

1

Interns got lost

We interviewed ongoing interns, and many were unhappy with the experience. They did not know whom to report to or who oversaw their internship. Inside institutions, no one was accountable. The program was new, and internships sat in different departments. Many had been posted only because of pressure from the ministry. They did not recommend TULIP to others, so the program did not spread by word of mouth.

2

Small cities did not know what an internship was

Officials in tier 3 cities had not used internship contracts before. Few people there knew the portal existed.

3

No one had a marketing budget

The central government gave TULIP no marketing budget, and small cities had none of their own. They also had no way to design posters.

II

Strategy and scoping

Product strategy

My goal was to scale TULIP. We used three levers: fix the product, market it, and roll it out through the states.

  1. Product: remove the bottlenecks that kept institutions from posting and interns from doing useful work.
  2. Marketing: make the portal known in small cities with channels they already had.
  3. State rollout: get state governments to push their cities to post internships.

We tracked three numbers through a Monitoring and Evaluation framework that I designed: internships posted, applications and placements.

The ministry's target for TULIP was 10,000 posted internships and 5,000 completed internships.

Product definition

New requirement

A mentor for every intern

Senior officials in each institution assigned a point of contact on the portal for each intern. That person became responsible for the intern through the internship.

ResultThis gave interns someone to report to and gave institutions clear accountability.
New check

Only genuine students could apply

Some departments had used dummy student accounts to meet their quota of applications. I added uploads of a student ID and an Aadhaar card at registration.

ResultThis stopped registrations by people who were not students.

Scaling and outcome

Launch and scaling

With no marketing budget, we reached small cities through channels they already had.

Workshops first. We ran workshops for city officials on what an internship is. We showed how interns can build a city's capacity by bringing in new ideas.

An outreach toolkit any city could use. I built a low-tech toolkit and sent it centrally to 4,500+ city governments:

Poster templates in MS WordEvery city office had a computer operator who knew Word. The graphics stayed fixed. The operator changed only the phone number, the type of internship and the office address. The city printed the posters and put them up in busy markets and local colleges.
Radio-style jinglesWe recorded our own jingles. Cities played them on the announcement systems of their municipal vehicles.
Placement guidanceAnd a standard circular explaining what to do.

Applications grew from 250+ to 7,200+ in 8 months.

State rollout for the supply side. Cities also had to post more internships. We worked through each state's urban department:

  1. We grouped the state's urban local bodies by size, with one WhatsApp group each for small, medium and large cities.
  2. In each city, we found the person with the authority to post internships and added them to the group.
  3. We shared sample internship postings from the center.
  4. We ran workshops on what internships a city could offer, how to post them on the portal and how to find the budget.

Coordination with senior officials. TULIP was voluntary, so cities acted only when the state asked them to. We reached the most senior officials in each state so they would issue orders to carry out the plan. We also set up regular meetings between the states and the central government.

Graduation ceremonies. We held graduation ceremonies for interns who completed their internships. This made TULIP more prestigious and more attractive to students.

Optimization

Funnel analysis raised placements 300% in 6 months.

Using the M&E framework, I analyzed user data to find where candidates dropped off in the application funnel. We then removed that friction.
