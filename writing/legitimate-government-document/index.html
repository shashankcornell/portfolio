Evaluating Large Language Models for Drafting Indian Government Documents

An AI model can write something that sounds exactly like a government order. Sounding right is not enough. This study asks what actually makes a government document valid, and builds a way to test whether AI drafts meet that bar.

AuthorsShashank Mishra and Dr. Pranav Gupta
StatusOngoing. Proposal and pilot stage.
Corpus3,609 documents from the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs
ContributionA six-part quality score for government documents

01

The question

Government runs on documents. Tenders, orders, memoranda, guidelines and project proposals carry decisions, money and legal weight.

Drafting them takes time and skill, and many offices are short of both. Large language models (LLMs) can produce formal, official-sounding text in seconds. So the obvious question is whether they can help officials draft.

But a government document is not just text in the right tone. It must state the right facts: the correct authority, amounts, dates and legal references. It must follow a fixed structure, with every required section present. It must comply with procedure, such as eligibility rules and evaluation methods. A tender with the wrong deposit amount or a missing annexure is not a slightly worse tender. It is unusable, and it can cause real harm.

FactsIs it correct?Departments, dates, amounts, legal references, designations.
FormIs it complete?Every section that kind of document requires, in the expected order.
ProcedureIs it compliant?The legal and procedural elements that make it valid.

So we start from a more basic question: what makes a government document legitimate? Our answer becomes a test. We then use that test to measure how well LLMs draft real Indian government documents, and what kind of help they need to do it well.

02

What we already know

We surveyed recent work on AI for legal and government writing. Five findings stand out.

Fitzpatrick et al., 2025It depends on the taskIn a field experiment, AI raised quality by 17% and speed by 34% on document tasks, but lowered quality by 12% on analytical tasks.
LegalCheck, Amsterdam; Swiss procurementDrafting support works with human oversightTools captured 80% to 100% of required content, and cut manual effort substantially in procurement.
Magesh et al., 2025Hallucination is still a real riskEven purpose-built legal AI tools invent facts, and accuracy varies by jurisdiction.
Wu et al., 2025Domain adaptation helps a lotA fine-tuned Gemma-27B outperformed GPT-4o on policy writing. Legal pretraining works at scale.
Across the literatureAccountability lags behind deploymentWho is the author, who is liable for errors, and where automated decisions should stop are all unresolved.
03

The gaps

Understudied

  • No work on Indian government documents such as office memoranda, D.O. letters and cabinet notes.
  • Whether AI follows procedure and institutional form is untested.
  • No rigorous evidence on LLMs as a tool for building staff capacity.
  • Drafting in multiple Indian languages is unaddressed.
  • Long-term effects are unknown. Do officials gain skill, or lose it?
→

Opportunity

  • 18 barriers to AI in Indian public systems are documented, but no LLM solutions have been tested.
  • Only one India-focused language tool for governance exists, DigiGram, at the panchayat level.
  • Proven methods are ready to apply: retrieval-augmented generation, domain fine-tuning and a human in the loop.
04

The corpus

Our documents come from the decommissioned SmartNet database of the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, which held material from India's urban missions. We collected 3,609 documents and classified them by type.

Tenders and procurement documents make up a third of the corpus. They are also the most rule-bound, which makes them a strong test.

05

The experiment

For each real document, we write a short pre-drafting brief: the facts an official would have before starting to write. We give the brief to an LLM, compare its draft with the real document, and score the difference.

1ClassifySort documents by type.
2MetadataRecord authority, purpose, amounts, dates.
3BriefsWrite a pre-drafting brief, in three conditions.
4GenerateDraft each document with the LLM.
5ScoreAutomatic scoring on six dimensions.
6Human reviewExperts rate usability.
7AnalyzeCompare conditions and document types.

The three conditions test how much the model needs to be told.

Condition 1Minimal promptThe basic facts and an instruction to write in formal Government of India language.
Condition 2Structured briefThe facts plus the required sections, the real eligibility rules, an instruction not to invent criteria, and a step where the model must ask for missing facts first.
Condition 3Guided institutional promptAdds guidance on the institution's own conventions and procedure.

Here is an example, a tender to build a non-motorized transport corridor for Jabalpur Smart City Limited.

Condition 1: minimal prompt
You are drafting an official government tender document Request For Proposal. Use the following information to prepare the RFP in formal government of India language. Document type: Request for Proposal Issuing authority: Jabalpur Smart City Limited Purpose: Hire a vendor to develop a NMT (non motorised transit) from Bus Stand to Madan Mohan Station Location: Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh Budget ceiling: INR 7 crore 43 lakhs Submission deadline: 30 days from publication
Condition 2: structured brief
You are drafting an official government tender document Request For Proposal. Use the following information to prepare the RFP in formal government of India language. Before generating the document, ask any missing facts and once you have all the information, then ask if you can generate the document. When I will permit, then generate. Use numbered clauses. Document type: Request for Proposal Issuing authority: Jabalpur Smart City Limited Purpose: Hire a vendor to develop a NMT (non motorised transit) from Bus Stand to Madan Mohan Station Location: Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh Budget ceiling: INR 7 crore 43 lakhs Submission deadline: The online bid submission deadline is 24 February 2017. Sections in the RFP: Section 1: NIT Section 2: Instructions to Bidders (ITB), Bid Data Sheet, Annexure A to M Section 3: Table of Clauses Part I: General Conditions of Contract (GCC), Contract Data, Annexure N to W Part II: Special Conditions of Contract (SCC) Section 4: Bill of Quantities (BOQ) Section 5: Agreement Form Do not invent eligibility criteria. Eligibility criteria are as follows: Pre-qualification is required. Special eligibility criteria is not required. The bidder must have completed similar works in the last 3 financial years, either: Three similar works, each worth at least 20% of the probable contract amount, or Two similar works, each worth at least 30% of the probable contract amount, or One similar work worth at least 50% of the probable contract amount.
06

How we score a draft

Most text metrics only check whether the words look similar. A draft can match the reference closely and still carry the wrong amount. So we score six dimensions, from surface to substance.

1Surface similarityROUGE-L, BLEU, BERTScore, cosine similarity.
2Structural completenessAre scope, eligibility, evaluation, payment and penalties all there?
3Factual accuracyDepartments, dates, amounts, legal references, designations.
4Legal and procedural complianceA checklist of required legal elements, each scored 0, 1 or 2.
5Bureaucratic stylePassive voice, deontic language ("shall", "must"), nominalizations, clause density.
6Human expert ratingA 5-point usability scale, judged by people who draft these documents.

The Government Document Quality Score

The automatic dimensions combine into one score, the GDQS. The weights follow the harm an error can do.

GDQS = 0.25 × Factual accuracy + 0.20 × Structural completeness + 0.20 × Legal compliance
       + 0.15 × Semantic similarity + 0.10 × Bureaucratic style + 0.10 × Readability
0.25Facts weigh mostErrors cause real administrative harm.
0.20 eachThen structure and complianceTemplates are rigid. A missing section makes a document unusable.
0.15 and 0.10Meaning, style, readabilityImportant, but lower risk if wrong.

07

What we expect

We will compare scores across document types such as tenders, contracts, policy memos, orders and financial documents. Three hypotheses guide the analysis.

H1Compliance is the weakest dimensionIt is the hardest to get right without grounding in the domain.
H2Policy memos score highestThey are closest to the general expository writing LLMs are good at.
H3Financial documents score lowestThey pack the most precise facts into the least space.
08

An early test

We ran the Jabalpur tender through a frontier model, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and had the same model score the result against the real document.

Factual accuracy1/5
Structure1/5
Compliance1/5
Style4/5
Overall1.8
  • The deposit amount was invented: INR 14,86,000 instead of Rs. 7,42,500.
  • The project scope was wrong: 3.80 km with 2 to 2.5 m tracks, against 1.2 km with 4 m cycle and 4 m pedestrian tracks.
  • The completion period became 18 months instead of 5.
  • The evaluation method changed from lowest bid (L1) to quality and cost based selection.
  • Yet the language was rated highly formal, with correct terms such as "hereinafter referred to as" and "shall be liable".

The draft looked and sounded like a real tender. It was not one.

That gap is the reason for this study. Style is the easiest thing for a model to copy and the least important thing for a document to be legitimate. Facts, structure and procedure are what make it valid, and they are exactly where the model failed. Our next steps are to run all three conditions across the corpus and bring in expert reviewers.
