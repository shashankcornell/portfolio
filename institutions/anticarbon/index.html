Anticarbon

An advertising company with one goal: to take plastic bags out of Delhi's local markets. Advertisers would pay for newspaper bags, made by women in Delhi's slums, so shops could hand them out for free.

01

The problem

Plastic bags were everywhere in Delhi's local markets. A better bag already existed, but it could not compete on price.

Women in the slums of Delhi made bags out of old newspapers, in different sizes. I worked with them and wanted to sell these bags in the market, so that people would use them instead of plastic bags. The problem was cost. Newspaper bags were expensive, and there was no way to subsidize them. Plastic bags were cheap and very useful, and newspaper bags could not beat them.

The openingThe Supreme Court banned thin plastic bags in Delhi

It was the push we needed. We seized the moment to get people to use newspaper bags instead. But the bags were still too expensive.

02

The idea: let advertisers pay for the bag

We printed advertisements on the newspaper bags. The ad money brought the cost of each bag down, so shopkeepers could get them for free, and a free bag can beat plastic on price.

Placing ads where the right customers shop

A physical market is well sorted: certain kinds of customers go to certain kinds of shops. So an advertiser could choose the local stores where its desired customers shop, and put its ad on the bags those stores hand out.

03

Why it didn't win

The idea did not scale. It died in the pilot. People never stopped using plastic bags.

Plastic is too useful

I underestimated how useful plastic bags are. They are dirt cheap and fit in a small space. After the ban, the market quickly found other ways to keep using them.

Bags that broke under real loads

Small local shops mostly sell everyday goods such as groceries. For heavy loads like vegetables, a newspaper bag had to be very big, and it could not carry anything liquid or likely to leak.

Only small local shops would take part

A shop that sells certain brands will not promote a different brand whose products it does not sell. That limited the idea to very local shops.

Not appealing to look at

The newspaper bags were not attractive enough for people to choose them over plastic.

Few paying clients

I did not know much about how to run a business at the time, and it was hard to win advertising clients.

04

The exit

I closed Anticarbon after the pilot. I sold all the newspaper bags to a not-for-profit. That let us break even and pay the women who had made the bags.
